Plain English for the things with odd names

The Concordance
The one-page sheet headed "A Reader's Concordance" with "The Prophet, by Kahlil Gibran" printed under the masthead. It gives one word-counting rule for each of 84 pages. She needs a real copy of The Prophet to use it; the sheet itself names the book, so put the book on the shelf among others and nothing more is needed.
Plate I and Plate II
The two circles on the cipher-wheel pages. Plate I, "The Dial", is the big one with clock numerals round the rim. Plate II, "The Hands", is the smaller one with two clock hands printed on it. Cut both out. Push a brad (split pin) through the centre of Plate I and tape it there; she pins Plate II on top and turns it until the hands read 3:30.
Pigpen
A cipher where each letter is drawn as the walls of the box it sits in. The printed Pig-Pen Alphabet sheet is the key. The only pigpen you write by hand is the wall; the flags' pigpen is already on the website.
Wheel symbols
Ordinary letters and digits that only mean something once the wheel is set. You copy them onto the cushion card exactly as printed below.

Lock and load, in this order, so no key ends up locked inside its own box

  1. Put the prize in the backpack's inner compartment. Padlock that compartment. Set its key aside: this is PURPLE.
  2. Close the backpack's main zip. Padlock it. Set its key aside: DARK BLUE.
  3. Put the backpack inside the suitcase. Padlock the suitcase. Set its key aside: BLACK.
  4. Leave the suitcase somewhere she will see it all evening. It is meant to taunt.
  5. Now hide the three keys: BLACK in a ziplock in the toilet cistern; DARK BLUE in the Dutch oven with Plate I; PURPLE taped flat behind the Milk Crate painting, under a sheet of paper, before you put that painting in her envelope.
  6. LIGHT BLUE and its lock are not used. Put them in a drawer.

Exactly what to write by hand, and where

The bathroom wall or mirror. In dry-erase marker, large: ALIEN BLUES. Directly beneath it, in the invisible-ink pen, copy the pigpen shapes from the tracing sheet (the page before this guide). They read BREATHE IN BREATHE OUT LOOK INSIDE. Test a corner first: glazed tile and glass wipe clean, grout and stone do not. The mirror is the safest surface.
The cushion card. On a quarter-sheet frame or any card, in capitals, exactly: MFF6 DW5OY7 H5W LO8QH 9KOYHOYA. Put it in a small envelope. On the envelope: This will mean nothing until the hour is set. Tuck it just inside the cushion's zip.
The six painting backs. Write on the quarter-sheet frames and attach one to each painting.
Milk Crate: The famous man we always see here, who is not him.
Patricia's: Just the name. Look her up.
Hamlet: We saw the film of this play. Whose film was it?
Fairy houses: What was the family name of the one who lived here?
Abyssinia: Find this country's colours at sarahs.quest, and turn them over.
Gleaners: The café sits in a market named for a country. Its colours are at sarahs.quest too.
The welcome letter. Your words. It must contain the address sarahs.quest, may mention that not everything can be read on the day it is found, and must end with the line breathe in, breathe out.
Nothing else is handwritten. The flags' pigpen is on the website. The register, the card guide, the concordance and the rest are printed and final.

Before she arrives: the five tests

  1. Put your real knife on your real scale. It must read 79 g. Nearest catalogue entries are 58 g and 99 g.
  2. Open your copy of The Prophet. Page 12, fourth word of the first line must be corn. Page 15, the first sentence beginning "Give", sixth word must be bread. If either fails, tell me the real page numbers and I will reprint the concordance.
  3. Shine the blue-light pen on the wall. The pigpen must show. Then wipe a corner to be sure it comes off.
  4. Pin the plates, set 3:30, decode the cushion card yourself. It must read LOOK BEHIND THE FIRST PAINTING.
  5. Open sarahs.quest on your phone, tap Plate VII, confirm the pigpen on the back reads HALF PAST THREE.
